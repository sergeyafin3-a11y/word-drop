Lesson 2

Have you ever…?

60 min

One tense, one job: you ask about a person’s life, not about a day. Three minutes of rules, then you use it.

Have you ever taken a yellow cab? — Yes, I have!

1 · One idea

LIFE · no date

some time before today. We don’t say when.
I have been to Paris.
She has never eaten sushi.

STORY · with a date

one day in the past. We say when.
I went to Paris last spring.
She ate sushi on Friday.

2 · The form: have / has + V3

+I / you / we / they have + V3 · he / she / it has + V3
I have seen it. · She has seen it.
−haven’t / hasn’t + V3
I haven’t seen it. · He hasn’t seen it.
?Have you ever + V3? · Has she ever + V3?
Have you ever seen it?
short answerYes, I have. · No, I haven’t. — never a long answer
V3 is the third form. Regular verbs just take -ed: watch → watched, text → texted.

3 · V3 of the verbs you actually say

be — beensee — seendo — doneeat — eaten go — beenmeet — mettake — takenwrite — written break — brokenwin — won

4 · Three small words

everonly in questions = “some time in your life”
Have you ever been to London?
nevermeans “not”, so you never add not
I have never been to London. (not: haven’t never)
alreadyearlier than expected
She has already watched the whole season.

5 · How to choose in one second

Is there a time word?yesterday · last week · in 2020 · at seven · on Friday
YESPast Simple: I saw it yesterday. · He texted me last night.
NOPresent Perfect: I have seen it. · He has texted me.
Wrong: I have seen it yesterday. Right: I saw it yesterday.

6 · The pattern of a real conversation

You: Have you ever been to London?
Me: Yes, I have.
You: When was that?
Me: Two years ago.
You: Who did you go with?
See it? The first question is Present Perfect — it opens the door. Everything after “Yes, I have” is Past Simple, because now we talk about that one day.

Six ways to give advice

You shouldthe normal one, friend to friend
You should talk to her today. · You shouldn’t wait.
Why don’t you…?softer — an idea, not an order
Why don’t you text him first?
I’d just…what I would do in your place
I’d just send one message.
I’d recommend…polite and a bit more serious · + verb -ing
I’d recommend talking to her face to face.
Have you tried…?when the person already did something · + verb -ing
Have you tried calling her?
If I were you…the strongest one — keep it for the end
If I were you, I would say sorry first.
— He never texts first. — Why don’t you text him today? If I were you, I would stop counting.

advice · a recommendation · a tip

advicewhat to do about a problem. One form for everything — never “advices”, never “an advice”.
Can you give me some advice? · One piece of advice: talk to her.
a recommendationwhat to choose: a film, a café, a book. You can count them.
Any recommendations for a series? · I have two recommendations.
a tipone small trick that works. You can count them too.
Here is a tip: text her in the morning.
Problems take advice. Choices take recommendations. Tips are short and practical.

And what do you say back?

You like the advice

That’s a good idea.
I’ll try that, thanks.

You don’t

I don’t think that will work.
Maybe later. I’m not ready.

Lesson 4

What do you think?

60 min

Today we learn to say what we think and to react — so that a conversation does not die after one word. “Good” is not an opinion.

One city, eight million people and nobody agrees about anything

Words for an opinion

a seriesa seasonan episodea song an albuma charactermy favouriteinteresting funnysadterriblegreat It’s not for meI really like it

1 · One rule, two fingers

👉 the thing → -ing

Point at the film, the song, the lesson.
The film is boring.
This song is amazing.

👈 me → -ed

Point at yourself. This is how you feel.
I am bored.
I am excited.

2 · Six pairs you need

boreThe lesson is boring. → I am bored.
interestThe book is interesting. → I am interested.
exciteThe concert is exciting. → I am excited.
surpriseThe ending is surprising. → I am surprised.
tireThe trip was tiring. → I was tired.
relaxThis music is relaxing. → I am relaxed.
The thing makes the feeling. The thing takes -ing, the person takes -ed.

3 · Careful! One letter changes everything

I am bored.I feel nothing interesting right now. ✅ what you want to say
I am boring.People are bored with me. I am a boring person. 😅
I am interested.I want to know more. ✅
I am interesting.I am an interesting person — maybe true, but not the answer.
After I am / he is / we were about a person, use -ed. Almost always.

4 · so and such

so + adjectiveIt’s so good. · She was so tired.
such + a + adjective + nounIt’s such a good song. · He is such a kind person.
the easy testIs there a noun after the adjective? Yes → such a. No → so.
— The last episode was so sad! — I know. It’s such a good series.

React, don’t just answer

surpriseNo way! · Are you serious? · I can’t believe it.
interestReally? Tell me more. · And then what happened?
agreeingExactly. · Same here. · That’s so true.
buying timeWell… · To be honest… · Let me think.
A dead conversation is a conversation with no reactions. No way! is always better than silence.
Task 3 · Do you like it? I name five things — a singer, a film, a phone, a school subject, a city. You answer with an opinion and a reason: I really like it, because… · It’s not for me, because…
Task 4 · The podcast. You are the guest, I am the host: What are you watching at the moment? · What’s the best thing you’ve seen this year? · What do people love that you don’t like? Every answer longer than one sentence, and at least one so or such in it. Then we swap.
Task 5 · Speaking. One day you land in a new country. What are you excited about, and what is a bit frightening? Useful: I’d love to live in… · I’m planning to… · The first thing I’d do is…
