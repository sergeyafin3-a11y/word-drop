Warm-up

Who is who

10 min

A short warm-up: you already know how to describe people, so here we only bring the four friends back and start talking.

the writer

Carrie

She writes for a newspaper about life in New York. She loves clothes and shoes. She talks a lot, she is romantic and always late.

the lawyer

Miranda

She is a lawyer with short red hair and dark clothes. Clever, strict and funny. She works too much and hates long conversations.

Lesson 2

Have you ever…?

60 min

This is the tense that opens stories. You ask Have you ever…?, the other person says yes — and then you both switch to the past.

Have you ever taken a yellow cab? — Yes! When? Where were you going?

Experience — no date

She has never been to Paris.
I have already seen this series.

Story — with a date

She went to Paris last spring.
I saw it in June.

have / has + third form

+I have seen it. · She has seen it.
−I haven’t seen it. · He hasn’t seen it.
?Have you ever seen it? · Has she ever been there?
neverI’ve never been to New York (no “not” with never)
with a dateyesterday, last year, in 2020 → Past Simple, not this tense
— Have you ever flown alone? — Yes, I have. — When was that? — Two years ago.

Third forms you will really use

be — beensee — seendo — doneeat — eaten go — gonemeet — mettake — takenwrite — written break — brokenwin — won
Task 1. Complete and then say the sentence out loud.
1(be) ___ you ever ___ abroad?
2(meet) ___ you ever ___ a famous person?
3(lose) I ___ never ___ my phone.
4(watch) She ___ already ___ the whole season.
5(text) He ___ me last night. careful — there is a time here
1 Have … been · 2 Have … met · 3 have … lost · 4 has … watched · 5 texted
Task 2 · Speaking game. Ask me ten questions with Have you ever…? Every time I say yes, ask When? Where? Who with? Your job is not the question — your job is to keep me talking.
Task 3 · Speaking. Three things you have already done this year, and three things you have never done but really want to.
Lesson 3

Friends and drama

60 min

Four friends argue, stop talking and make up again in every season. These are the words for exactly that — and they work for real life too.

A park bench, two friends and one long apology

When it goes wrong

They had a big argument.
She didn’t text me back.
He forgot about me.

When it gets better

I said sorry first.
We were friends again the same day.
She always helps me.
a best friendto be good friendsto be upset to be angryto say sorryto be friends again to keep a secretto tell the truth
Task 1. Put the story in order, then tell it out loud in five sentences.
AThey didn’t talk for a week.
BThey were best friends for six years.
CThen one of them forgot the other’s birthday.
DIn the end she said sorry, and they were friends again.
EThey had a big argument about it.
B → C → E → A → D

Advice like a friend, not like a book

shouldYou should talk to her. · You shouldn’t wait.
softerWhy don’t you call her? · Maybe you could text first.
honestTo be honest, I think you were wrong.
— She never says sorry first. — To be honest? You should talk to her anyway.
Task 2 · Give advice. I read you a problem — you answer with three different pieces of advice, then say which one you would take yourself.

Role A · the upset friend

Your best friend told somebody your secret. You are upset and you don’t want to talk to them. Explain what happened.

Role B · the honest friend

You think they should talk. Be kind, but say it. Use you should, why don’t you, to be honest.

Lesson 4

What do you think?

60 min

Today we learn to say what we think and to react — so that a conversation does not die after one word. “Good” is not an opinion.

One city, eight million people and nobody agrees about anything

Words for an opinion

a seriesa seasonan episodea song an albuma charactermy favouriteinteresting funnysadterriblegreat It’s not for meI really like it

The thing → -ing

what it is like
The show was boring.
Her new album is amazing.

Me → -ed

how I feel
I was so bored.
I’m really excited about it.

-ed, -ing, so and such

-ingthe thing: boring film · exciting news · surprising ending
-edthe person: I’m bored · I’m excited · I was surprised
soso + adjective: It’s so good.
suchsuch + a + adjective + noun: It’s such a good song.
— The last episode was so sad! — I know. It’s such a good series.

Say it like you mean it

When you like it

It’s my favourite episode.
You should watch it!
I could watch it again and again.

Honest opinions

Honestly? I don’t like it much.
It’s not for me.
I really like this band.

React, don’t just answer

surpriseNo way! · Are you serious? · I can’t believe it.
interestReally? Tell me more. · And then what happened?
agreeingExactly. · Same here. · That’s so true.
buying timeWell… · To be honest… · Let me think.
A dead conversation is a conversation with no reactions. No way! is always better than silence.
Task 3 · Do you like it? I name five things — a singer, a film, a phone, a school subject, a city. You answer with an opinion and a reason: I really like it, because… · It’s not for me, because…
Task 4 · The podcast. You are the guest, I am the host: What are you watching at the moment? · What’s the best thing you’ve seen this year? · What do people love that you don’t like? Every answer longer than one sentence, and at least one so or such in it. Then we swap.
Task 5 · Speaking. One day you land in a new country. What are you excited about, and what is a bit frightening? Useful: I’d love to live in… · I’m planning to… · The first thing I’d do is…
