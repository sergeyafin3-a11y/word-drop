Lesson 1

The Cast: who is who

60 min

Speak first, be correct later. In this lesson mistakes are normal — we only fix the words we are learning today.

✍️The Writer

Carrie-type

In her thirties. Curly hair, huge shoes, huger problems. Talkative and romantic.

⚖️The Lawyer

Miranda-type

Short red hair, dark suits. Clever, sarcastic, always busy, never impressed.

💅The Publicist

Samantha-type

Blonde, loud, confident. Knows everybody in town and is afraid of nothing.

🖼️The Art Dealer

Charlotte-type

Dark hair, perfect manners. Polite, romantic, a little old-fashioned.

Words you need

What does she look like?

outside: face, hair, clothes
She’s tall, with curly blonde hair.

What is she like?

inside: character
She’s confident and very funny.

Two questions people always mix up

look likeappearance — hair, height, style
be likecharacter — funny, kind, difficult
word orderShe’s a tall young woman with dark hair
— What’s your best friend like? — Honestly? Loud, but the kindest person I know.
Practice 1. Say one sentence about each character: first appearance, then character. Use with: She’s a short woman with red hair.

Speaking. Describe three people you know — a friend, someone in your family, and a person you follow online. Six sentences each. I will guess who is who.
Speaking · the big one. Which character are you most like, and why? Start like this: I think I’m like the lawyer, because…
Lesson 2

Have you ever…?

60 min

This is the tense you need for real conversation: it opens stories. You ask Have you ever…?, the other person says yes, and then you both switch to the past.

Experience — no date

She has never been to Paris.
I have already met his family.

Story — with a date

She went to Paris last spring.
I met his family in June.

have / has + third form

+I have seen it. · She has seen it.
−I haven’t seen it. · He hasn’t seen it.
?Have you ever seen it? · Has she ever been there?
neverI’ve never been to New York. (no “not” with never)
with datenever Present Perfect: yesterday, last year, in 2020 → Past Simple
— Have you ever been in love? — Yes, I have. — When was that? — Two years ago.

Third forms you will actually use

be — beensee — seendo — doneeat — eaten go — gonemeet — mettake — takenwrite — written break — brokenfall — fallen
Practice. Make a question with Have you ever…? and answer it honestly. If the answer is yes — add one sentence in the past.
1(be) ___ you ever ___ abroad?
2(meet) ___ you ever ___ a famous person?
3(lose) I ___ never ___ my phone.
4(watch) She ___ already ___ the whole season.
5(text) He ___ me last night. (careful — there is a time here)
1 Have … been · 2 Have … met · 3 have … lost · 4 has … watched · 5 texted
Speaking game · Find out about me. Ask me ten questions with Have you ever…? Every time I say yes, ask When? Where? Who with? Your job is not the question — your job is to keep me talking.
Speaking · your list. Three things you have already done this year, and three things you have never done but really want to.
Lesson 3

Dating, drama and advice

60 min

Today we learn the phrases people really use when they talk about each other — and how to give advice without sounding like a textbook.

The good part

He asked her out.
They went on a date.
They’re seeing each other.

The drama

He didn’t text her back.
They broke up last week.
Then they made up again.
a datea crusha red flagto flirt to ghost somebodyto be singleto be in a relationship to move in togetherto argueto apologise
Practice 1. Put the story in order. Say the whole story out loud when the order is right.
AShe waited three days. Nothing.
BThey met at a party in September.
CHe asked her out the same night.
DIn the end he called and apologised.
EAfter the date he didn’t text her back.
B → C → E → A → D

Giving advice like a friend, not like a book

shouldYou should talk to her. · You shouldn’t wait.
softerWhy don’t you call him? · Maybe you could text her first.
strongHonestly? Just forget about it.
— He never texts first. — Girl. That’s a red flag. You should move on.
Speaking · the advice column. I read you a problem, you answer with three different pieces of advice. Then say which one you would take yourself.

Role A · the friend in love

You met somebody last week. You like them too much. You want advice — and you don’t want to hear anything bad about them.

Role B · the honest friend

You think this person is a red flag. Be kind, but say it. Use you should, why don’t you, to be honest.

Lesson 4

Red carpet English

60 min

Small talk is a skill, and it is the same at the Grammys and at a new school in a new country: compliment, question, reaction, question again.

🎤The interview

Who are you wearing?

Thirty seconds, four questions, a thousand cameras.

👗The look

I’m obsessed with this dress

Compliments are the fastest way into a conversation.

🏆The award

I can’t believe it

Reactions: surprise, joy, “I didn’t expect this”.

Compliments that work

You look amazing tonight.
I’m obsessed with your jacket.
That colour really suits you.
— Thank you! That’s so nice of you.
— Oh, stop it. But thank you!

Interview questions

Q: So, how do you feel right now?
Q: What are you working on at the moment?
Q: Have you ever been here before?
Q: What’s next for you?

React, don’t just answer

surpriseNo way! · Are you serious? · I can’t believe it.
interestReally? Tell me more. · And then what happened?
agreeingExactly. · Same here. · That’s so true.
buying timeWell… · To be honest… · Let me think.
A dead conversation is a conversation with no reactions. Say something, anything — No way! is better than silence.
Speaking · the red carpet. You are the star, I am the reporter. Answer four questions, and every answer must be longer than one sentence. Then we swap: you interview me.
Speaking · moving out. One day you land in a new country. Tell me: where would you go, what would you do first, who would you talk to? Useful: I’d love to live in… · I’m planning to… · The first thing I’d do is…
